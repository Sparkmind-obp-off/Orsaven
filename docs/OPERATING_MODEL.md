# ORSAVEN — Operating Model

## Product lifecycle

1. Discover a recurring customer problem.
2. Validate demand with conversations, search intent, or small experiments.
3. Define a product brief and measurable acceptance criteria.
4. Confirm ownership, source rights, provider terms, and data implications.
5. Build or package the smallest useful version.
6. Test delivery, documentation, compatibility, and support paths.
7. Approve the product for its maturity label.
8. Publish accurate product information.
9. Monitor customer feedback, defects, refunds, and costs.
10. Update, pause, or retire based on evidence.

## Quality gates

A product cannot be marked Available until:
- Scope and deliverables are explicit.
- Rights and licenses are reviewed.
- Setup and delivery have been tested end to end.
- Dependencies and recurring costs are disclosed.
- Security and privacy implications are documented.
- Support and refund responsibilities are assigned.
- Versioning and rollback/retirement paths exist.

## Customer support

Use a consistent support intake with order/reference, product version, environment, reproduction steps, expected result, and actual result. Never request passwords, private keys, or unnecessary sensitive information.

## Third-party dependencies

List provider, purpose, account requirements, applicable terms, data sent, expected cost, and service limitations. A provider outage or policy change must not be represented as an ORSAVEN guarantee.

## Governance

Maintain a decision log, product register, issue register, release notes, and incident record. Separate internal operational documentation from customer-facing materials.
